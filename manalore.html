First lessons

Importance

A 0 to 100 power score. Higher means the card shapes games and shows up in real decks. 80+ is a cornerstone.

Demand

How wanted a card is right now, driven by how often it actually gets played. High demand supports a higher price.

90d Outlook

Where the price could drift over three months if demand and supply hold. A guide, not a guarantee.

Supply

Cards that can never be reprinted have a price floor. Freely reprinted cards can fall when more copies are printed.

How the score is built

Every card is broken into six signals scored 0 to 10: play-rate (how often it shows up in real decks), efficiency (power for its cost), card advantage, flexibility, format breadth, and keyword density. Roles, threat, removal, counter, ramp and so on, are read straight from each card's rules text.

importance = 100 × ( 0.26·playRate + 0.18·efficiency + 0.14·cardAdvantage + 0.12·flexibility + 0.18·breadth + 0.12·keywords ) / 10

The bars on each card sheet show every signal's contribution, so you always see why a score landed where it did.

Price outlook

Demand drives price. The demand index is led by play-rate, then supply pressure pulls the other way.

demand = 100 × ( 0.55·playRate + 0.25·flexibility + 0.20·cardAdvantage ) / 10 drift/mo = (demand/100 − 0.50)·0.060 − (supplyRisk/10)·0.020 proj90d = priceNow × (1 + 3·drift/mo)

Confidence rises with play-rate (a thicker, more liquid market) and falls with supply risk. The outlook deliberately does not price in rules-update shocks or surprise reprints, the two largest sources of real-world error.

How the Forge builds a deck

The Forge studies the most-played lists for your archetype and colors, then tunes them: lower-impact cards are swapped for higher-importance picks the model rates above the field, fitted to a clean curve. It then names the deck from its colors and character. You get a real, legal skeleton you can build on, not a random pile.
